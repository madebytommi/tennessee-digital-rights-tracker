## Research and legal limitations

This entry documents publicly reported political strategy and independently verifiable organizational activity. It does not establish that the strategy described at a 2026 gathering in Rome has been implemented in Tennessee.

The project has not identified evidence showing that a Tennessee candidate, campaign, political action committee, sheriff's campaign, local political organization, or locally branded news outlet coordinated with the people described here. It has also not identified evidence that the speakers funded or directed a Tennessee election effort, or that the strategy affected a Tennessee election result.

Several of the most significant claims about Kevin DeAnna's remarks come from journalist Phil Williams's August 19 report and the video he reviewed. This project independently confirmed the gathering, the participants, the political-organizing context, the donor-pitch program, and the Tennessee connections described below, but did not independently produce a complete transcript of the relevant video. Those claims are therefore attributed to Williams rather than presented as independently transcribed statements.

This entry also does not establish any organizational, financial, or operational connection between the individuals described here and Metric Media or other Tennessee-focused pseudo-local publishing networks.

## What happened?

On August 19, 2026, investigative journalist Phil Williams published a report about political-organizing discussions at a private Counter-Currents retreat held earlier in the year in Rome, Italy.

Counter-Currents' own account confirms that it has organized private, invitation-only retreats for writers, donors, and associates since 2011 and intentionally keeps attendance small to encourage networking and collaboration. Its account of the 2026 Rome retreat identifies Kevin DeAnna and Jared Taylor among the participants and says the program included a panel in which participants made short pitches about why wealthy donors should support their movement.

Williams reports that DeAnna argued that the decline of local journalism creates an opportunity for white-nationalist political organizing. According to Williams, DeAnna proposed focusing political-action and get-out-the-vote efforts on rural white voters, argued that relatively low turnout makes some local elections inexpensive to influence, and advocated electing sheriffs aligned with the so-called constitutional-sheriff movement.

Williams also reports that Jared Taylor argued that relatively small amounts of money can materially affect some mayoral, state-house, and city-council races.

The significance for this Tracker is not the speakers' ideology by itself. It is the documented discussion of weakened local information infrastructure, political-message distribution, rural voter targeting, and low-participation elections as components of a strategy for building political power.

## What the primary source says

### Counter-Currents confirms the gathering and political-organizing program

Counter-Currents' April 2026 account confirms that the Rome retreat occurred, that attendance was intentionally limited, that Kevin DeAnna and Jared Taylor participated, and that the program included donor pitches and political-organizing discussions. It also says the retreat took place at the Rome headquarters of CasaPound.

A later Counter-Currents archive entry describes DeAnna's June 2026 political-organizing presentation as a tactical discussion of political technology and entryism. Counter-Currents separately published Jared Taylor's donor pitch from the same retreat and described it as an appeal for a billionaire to support what the site calls white survival.

These first-party records independently establish that the gathering involved movement-building, donor cultivation, networking, and political organization. They do not independently establish every statement attributed to DeAnna or Taylor in Williams's report.

### Local journalism is declining

The underlying local-news problem is independently documented. Northwestern University's Medill Local News Initiative reported in 2025 that 213 U.S. counties had no locally based news source and another 1,524 had only one, leaving roughly 50 million people with limited or no access to local news. Medill also found that rural and less-affluent communities were disproportionately underserved by new digital-news startups.

Medill separately documented rapid growth in local-news sites operated as parts of larger national networks. That trend does not establish that any particular network is deceptive or politically coordinated, but it makes ownership, production methods, sponsorship, and source provenance increasingly important research questions.

### Constitutional-sheriff ideology

The Southern Poverty Law Center describes constitutional-sheriff ideology as a county-supremacy theory asserting that county sheriffs possess ultimate law-enforcement authority and may disregard state or federal authority. SPLC traces the modern movement to older Posse Comitatus ideas.

Williams reports that DeAnna invoked this movement while discussing local electoral power. This project has not identified evidence that a specific Tennessee sheriff campaign was connected to that discussion.

## What advocates and critics say

Counter-Currents describes its retreats as opportunities to build relationships, collaboration, political capacity, and donor support for its movement.

Williams's report argues that the decline of local journalism can create an accountability gap that extremist political organizers may seek to exploit, particularly in rural communities with fewer professional reporters covering local government and elections.

The Southern Poverty Law Center identifies Kevin DeAnna as a white-nationalist organizer and writer whose work has focused in part on embedding movement ideas within broader political institutions. SPLC also treats constitutional-sheriff ideology as part of a broader anti-government and county-supremacy tradition.

The Tracker does not treat political labels as evidence by themselves. In this entry, the characterization of DeAnna and the movement context is supported by first-party Counter-Currents materials, DeAnna's documented publication history, and the cited SPLC background research.

## Privacy and civil-liberties significance

This issue belongs in the Tracker because the central concern is not political extremism considered solely as ideology. It is the relationship among information infrastructure, political organizing, source provenance, and local democratic participation.

When professional local reporting disappears, residents may increasingly encounter civic information through social media, advocacy organizations, campaign communications, automated publishing networks, or locally branded sites whose ownership and production methods are not obvious to readers.

That creates recurring civic-information questions: Who produced the information? Who paid for it? Was it independently reported or repackaged from political material? Is apparently local content produced locally? Is paid, automated, or coordinated distribution being used to amplify it?

Those questions apply regardless of political ideology. Evidence that a strategy was discussed does not establish that it was implemented or successful, but the documented discussion provides a legitimate basis for monitoring Tennessee's civic-information infrastructure.

## Lawful actions and resources

- Support and follow independent local journalism in your community.
- Check who owns and operates unfamiliar locally branded news websites.
- Distinguish original reporting from articles based primarily on campaign releases or social-media posts.
- Review campaign-finance filings when political sponsorship or coordination is suspected.
- Preserve public political advertisements and webpages with their original context and disclosures.
- Attend local government and election meetings and review publicly available agendas and records.
- Submit documentary evidence or corrections to the Tracker without submitting sensitive personal information.
