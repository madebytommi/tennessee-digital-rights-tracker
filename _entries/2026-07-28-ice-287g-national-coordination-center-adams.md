## Research and legal limitations

This entry is public-record research, not legal advice. It describes what federal and Tennessee public records establish and separates awarded activities, contractor hiring, procurement requirements, and earlier planning documents.

The available public record does **not** establish every database, software platform, search tool, retention rule, or data-sharing pathway used by the 287(g) National Coordination Center. It also does not establish that every function described in a solicitation, request for information, or job posting has been implemented.

The project does not publish a precise facility address or security-sensitive operational details. The federal award initially listed Adams, Tennessee as the performance city. By August 20, 2026, reporting based on the updated USAspending record showed the place of performance had changed to **Nashville, Tennessee**, with ZIP code 37214. The precise property or lease remains unresolved.

## What happened?

On July 28, 2026, U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement awarded Koniag Services, Inc. a federal task order supporting Enforcement and Removal Operations' **287(g) National Coordination Center**. The award has a potential value of about $63 million if options are exercised, and performance began August 6, 2026.

When this entry was first published on August 17, the award record listed **Adams, Tennessee** as the performance city. That location quickly became disputed. On August 18, Montgomery County Mayor Wes Golden told NewsChannel 5 that federal contacts had told him there would be no ICE facility in Montgomery County or the Adams area.

By August 20, Tennessee Lookout reported that the federal USAspending record itself had changed the place of performance from Adams to **Nashville, Tennessee**, with ZIP code 37214, covering the Donelson area near Nashville International Airport. The current tracker therefore treats Adams as the award's **original, superseded location record** and Nashville as the **current federally reported place of performance**.

The location correction does not identify a precise building. Nashville officials told Tennessee Lookout they were still trying to get a consistent description of what is planned, and ICE had not provided a precise site publicly.

Koniag hiring provides additional evidence that implementation work is active in the Nashville area. A Facility Manager posting describes responsibility for the design, leasing, buildout, operational readiness, and day-to-day operation of a secure **40,000-60,000 square-foot Level II facility** supporting **24/7/365 call-center operations**. Another on-site Nashville Intelligence Analyst posting describes support for **critical missing-persons investigative operations**, including analysis of law-enforcement databases, public records, social-media/open-source information, and coordination with law-enforcement partners.

Those hiring records strengthen the evidence that a Nashville-area operation is actively being staffed and developed. They do not prove that every planned NCC cell or capability is operational.

Separate DHS and ICE procurement records describe an NCC intended to support the federal 287(g) program nationally. A 2026 transportation solicitation places required centralized dispatch operations inside the greater-Nashville NCC. Its Performance Work Statement calls for real-time GPS tracking of transport vehicles, electronic transport records, master dispatch logs, and a searchable contractor-hosted portal that may contain names, A-numbers, dates of birth, pickup and delivery locations, dates and times, and incident information.

That transportation solicitation was amended through A0003 on August 11, 2026, with proposals due August 12. As of this review, no reliable public award notice was located, so those transportation and portal functions remain **solicited requirements**, not confirmed live operations.

An earlier 2025 ICE Request for Information described a planned **287(g) Program National Call Center** in the greater Nashville area. That planning document contemplated electronic and telephone inquiries from 287(g) partners, data analysis, database cross-checks, enforcement confirmation, reporting dashboards, and specialized operational cells.

The public record does not yet explicitly state whether the 2025 **National Call Center** was renamed or incorporated into the 2026 **National Coordination Center**. The overlapping location, acronym, functions, and current Nashville hiring make continuity increasingly plausible, but the tracker does not treat a formal rename as established without an authoritative source.

Tennessee's connection extends beyond the federal award. Public Chapter 1015 requires each Tennessee county sheriff, by January 1, 2027, to enter into an agreement under an available federal 287(g) program. The Tennessee Centralized Immigration Enforcement Division says it coordinates state and local participation in federal immigration programs, including 287(g), and publishes a changing list of participating counties.

That statewide participation does **not** by itself establish that Tennessee agencies send inquiries or records to the Nashville NCC.

## What the primary source says

### The NCC support-services award

Federal award `70CDCR26FR0000034` states that the purpose of the task order is to provide support services for ICE Enforcement and Removal Operations' **287(g) National Coordination Center**.

The award identifies:

- Koniag Services, Inc. as the recipient;
- July 28, 2026 as the award date;
- August 6, 2026 as the start of performance; and
- about $63 million as the potential total value including options.

The award's place-of-performance metadata changed after award. The record initially listed Adams, Tennessee. By August 20, 2026, the updated federal record was reported as listing **Nashville, Tennessee / 37214**.

The award establishes a Tennessee and now Nashville-area connection to NCC support services. It does not by itself identify the exact property, lease arrangement, operational systems, or specific work performed by individual NCC personnel.

### Centralized dispatch and transport records

A DHS acquisition forecast and ICE transportation solicitation describe centralized 24/7/365 dispatch operations in the greater-Nashville NCC.

The transportation PWS requires functions including:

- real-time GPS tracking of transport vehicles;
- direct communication with field transport teams;
- electronic ICE Form G-391 generation and transmission;
- caller identity or credential verification;
- tracking through pickup and delivery;
- an electronic master log of transport requests;
- timestamps and location information; and
- daily operational reporting.

The contractor-hosted transport portal may contain:

- a detainee's name;
- A-number;
- date of birth;
- pickup and delivery locations;
- dates and times; and
- incident details.

The PWS describes that portal as a stand-alone contractor-hosted system that does not connect to or integrate with a government information system. It also prohibits biometric and classified information from being stored in that portal.

That limitation applies to the **transport portal described in this solicitation**. It does not establish what databases or systems the broader NCC may use.

Amendment A0003 was posted August 11 and made minor changes to submission and evaluation language, with the updated offer deadline on August 12. No reliable public award notice was located in the August 22 sweep. These transportation and portal functions are therefore described as **solicited contract requirements**, not confirmed live operations.

### Earlier Call Center planning and newer hiring evidence

ICE's 2025 National Call Center RFI described a planned greater-Nashville operation receiving approximately 6,000-7,000 calls per day and supporting electronic and telephone inquiries from 287(g) partners.

The planning document contemplated:

- data analysis and database cross-checks;
- confirmation of enforcement actions in the field;
- a 24/7 partner-support cell;
- an Integration and Location Cell involving unaccompanied children;
- an Integration and Automation Cell associated with the non-detained docket;
- a 287(g) Agreement Support Cell maintaining reports and dashboards; and
- a Technology Innovation and Rapid Fielding Cell intended to identify and field emerging technologies.

The RFI also asked vendors what technology they would recommend for integrating partner and noncitizen data with ICE systems.

Koniag's August 2026 Nashville hiring now provides implementation evidence consistent with portions of that earlier planning. The Facility Manager posting describes a secure 40,000-60,000 square-foot, 24/7/365 Nashville-area call-center facility. The Intelligence Analyst posting describes on-site Nashville missing-persons investigative support and analysis across law-enforcement databases, public records, social media, and other open-source tools.

Those postings are evidence of contractor staffing and facility-development activity. They do **not** by themselves prove that the entire National Call Center RFI was implemented, that the missing-persons position belongs to a formally activated Integration and Location Cell, or that every listed data source is part of the operational NCC architecture.

## What officials say

ICE describes the 287(g) program as a partnership through which state and local law-enforcement agencies receive delegated immigration-enforcement authority under federal supervision. ICE presents the program as a way to strengthen coordination between federal and local law enforcement.

Tennessee's Centralized Immigration Enforcement Division says it coordinates state and local participation in federal immigration programs, including 287(g), and facilitates communication among federal, state, and local agencies.

Montgomery County Mayor Wes Golden said on August 18 that federal contacts told him there would be no ICE facility in Montgomery County or the Adams area. Two days later, reporting showed the federal award location had changed to Nashville.

A spokesperson for Nashville Mayor Freddie O'Connell told Tennessee Lookout that the city was still trying to obtain a consistent description of what is planned. ICE had not publicly provided a precise facility address in the reporting reviewed for this update.

## What advocates and critics say

Civil-liberties groups have criticized expansion of the 287(g) program. In February 2026, the American Civil Liberties Union argued that expansion into routine local policing increases risks of racial profiling, civil-rights violations, fear of local law enforcement, and blurred accountability between federal and local agencies.

Government oversight has also identified management concerns. The U.S. Government Accountability Office reported in 2021 that ICE lacked performance measures covering all program activities and, at that time, lacked an adequate oversight mechanism for Warrant Service Officer participants. GAO's current recommendation status says ICE later implemented a WSO inspection mechanism, while other program-management recommendations remain open.

These national concerns do not establish misconduct at the Tennessee NCC or by Tennessee agencies. They show why access controls, audit practices, correction mechanisms, data retention, and the division of responsibility between ICE and local partners are important public-accountability questions.

- The award was signed July 28, 2026, and performance began August 6, 2026.
- The award initially listed Adams, Tennessee as the performance city.
- By August 20, the federal award record had been updated to **Nashville, Tennessee / 37214** as the reported place of performance.
- Montgomery County's mayor said federal contacts told him no ICE facility was planned for Montgomery County or the Adams area.
- Koniag has advertised Nashville-area positions for a Facility Manager overseeing design, leasing, buildout and operation of a secure 40,000-60,000 square-foot 24/7/365 call-center facility.
- Koniag has advertised an on-site Nashville Intelligence Analyst position supporting missing-persons investigative operations.
- DHS procurement records separately describe a greater-Nashville NCC.
- A separate transportation solicitation requires centralized dispatch operations inside the NCC if awarded and implemented as written.
- That transportation PWS requires real-time GPS tracking, electronic transport records, master logs, and a searchable secure portal containing specified personal identifiers.
- The PWS describes the transportation portal as stand-alone from government information systems and prohibits storage of biometric or classified information in that portal.
- The original 2025 National Call Center RFI contemplated data analysis, database cross-checks, partner inquiries, dashboards, specialized cells, and technology-fielding functions.
- Tennessee law requires each county sheriff to enter into an agreement under an available 287(g) program by January 1, 2027.

## Who may be affected?

The center's work may affect people encountered through participating 287(g) agencies and ICE enforcement operations, including people whose identity, immigration status, custody status, or transportation is researched or coordinated through program workflows.

Potentially affected groups include:

- people held by participating state or local agencies;
- people whose biographic identifiers are handled through 287(g) partner inquiries described in federal planning records;
- missing or unaccompanied people whose cases may be analyzed through contractor-supported investigative work;
- people transported in ICE custody under any transportation requirement that is ultimately awarded and implemented;
- state and local officers who submit inquiries or act on federal responses; and
- Tennessee residents and local agencies as the state expands participation in 287(g).

The existence of a record or database match does not by itself establish that the underlying information is accurate, current, or sufficient for a particular enforcement action.

## Privacy and civil-liberties significance

The significance of the NCC is not merely that ICE tied a federal 287(g) operation to Tennessee. The procurement record describes **centralization of data-supported immigration-enforcement coordination** in the Nashville area at the same time Tennessee is expanding local participation in 287(g).

The August location correction also illustrates a transparency problem: the award initially identified Adams, local officials said no such facility was coming there, and the federal database was subsequently changed to Nashville without a precise public explanation of the final site.

Koniag's Nashville hiring provides stronger evidence that a substantial secure facility is actively being designed, leased, built out, staffed, or prepared for operations. The missing-persons analyst posting also indicates that contractor-supported investigative work in Nashville may involve law-enforcement databases, public records, social media, and other open-source information. What systems those analysts can actually access through NCC operations remains unresolved.

Centralization can make partner support and enforcement coordination faster and more consistent. It can also concentrate sensitive identity, custody, location, investigative, and law-enforcement information into workflows whose implementation is difficult for the public to examine.

The transportation PWS gives a separate concrete example of the kinds of records contemplated for one part of the broader operation. A transport record may include identity information, an A-number, date of birth, pickup and destination locations, timestamps, requesting-agency information, and incident details. GPS requirements would also create a real-time record of transport movement if that solicitation is awarded and implemented as written.

The most defensible current conclusion is:

> ICE has awarded support services for a national 287(g) coordination center in Tennessee. The award initially listed Adams but was later updated to Nashville, while Koniag hiring provides evidence of active Nashville-area facility development and investigative staffing. Federal procurement records separately describe partner support, database cross-checks, dispatch, GPS-tracked transportation, and records containing personal identifiers, but the public record is still insufficient to determine the center's precise property, full database access, retention practices, correction processes, or direct data-sharing relationship with Tennessee agencies.

## Lawful actions and resources

- Review the federal NCC support-services award and its current place-of-performance information.
- Review Tennessee Public Chapter 1015 and the current CIED 287(g) participation page.
- Ask ICE for non-security-sensitive records describing the reason for the place-of-performance change, NCC privacy governance, records retention, access controls, audit policy, correction/redress procedures, and aggregate operational statistics.
- Ask Tennessee CIED and participating agencies for policies, MOUs, training materials, and aggregate records that show whether and how they interact with the NCC.
- Request aggregate information rather than individual immigration, custody, transport, missing-persons, or investigative records that could expose sensitive information about private people.
- Ask elected officials and oversight bodies to clarify what state or local information is sent to centralized federal systems and what safeguards apply to inaccurate or outdated information.
- For individual immigration-enforcement or constitutional-rights questions, consult a qualified attorney or accredited immigration legal-services provider.
