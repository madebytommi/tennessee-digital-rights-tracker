## Research and legal limitations

This entry is public-record research, not legal advice. Public Chapter 832 creates a statewide reporting and parent-notification duty, but it does not prescribe a statewide database, student-information-system field, electronic form, retention period, or technical workflow.

The available public record does not establish that every covered request becomes a permanent education record, that every Tennessee school district uses the same process, or that Knox County Schools uses an Aspen profile flag or automated gender-identity alert for Public Chapter 832. The Knox County implementation memorandum discussed below documents an email-and-form process, but it does not resolve the form's full access, retention, deletion, correction, or school-to-school transfer lifecycle.

A publicly available copy of the Knox County memorandum is hosted by a news organization rather than on the school district's current website. The Tracker cites it as a government-authored implementation document made public by a third party and does not re-host it or reproduce student-specific information.

## What happened?

Governor Bill Lee signed Senate Bill 1810 on May 1, 2024. The measure became Public Chapter 832 and took effect the same day.

The law applies to Tennessee local education agencies and public charter schools. It requires a school employee who receives a covered request from a student to report the request to a school administrator. The administrator must then report the request to the student's parent.

The law also prohibits school employees from knowingly providing false or misleading information to a parent about a student's gender identity or intention to transition to a gender different from the student's sex as specified in the statute.

The General Assembly's bill history records final passage in the Senate on March 21, 2024, by a vote of 23-6 and in the House on April 15, 2024, by a vote of 73-23 with one member present and not voting. The Senate later concurred in the House amendment on April 17, 2024.

## What the primary source says

Public Chapter 832 is codified at Tenn. Code Ann. § 49-6-315.

The statute covers requests by a student for an accommodation intended to affirm the student's gender identity. Its examples include a request to use a name that differs from the name on school registration forms or in the student's educational record, or a pronoun that does not correspond with the sex listed on the student's specified birth certificate.

The current codified structure separates several duties and remedies:

- subsection (a) prohibits knowingly giving a parent false or misleading information about the student's gender identity or intention to transition;
- subsection (b) creates the employee-to-administrator reporting duty and the administrator-to-parent reporting duty;
- subsection (c) creates a private civil action for a parent of an affected student, including specified remedies and attorney-fee provisions;
- subsection (d) authorizes the Tennessee Attorney General and Reporter to bring an action to enforce compliance; and
- subsection (e) contains definitions, including a definition of "student" limited to a person under 18.

The statute itself does not establish a statewide parental-consent form, centralized tracking database, electronic reporting portal, database flag, SIS workflow, or records-retention schedule.

## What officials say

The official legislative synopsis describes the law as requiring covered accommodation requests to be reported to a school administrator and parent and as authorizing civil enforcement by parents and the Tennessee Attorney General.

Knox County Schools and the Knox County Law Director's Office later issued an August 6, 2024 implementation memorandum for school administrators. The memorandum sets out a local "Required Best Practice" process that includes staff reporting to the principal, administrator discussion with the student, parent contact, and completion of a "Title IX Gender Identity Accommodation Form."

Knox County's guidance also includes a parental-permission step before the requested accommodation is granted. That is a Knox County implementation choice described in the local memorandum; § 49-6-315 itself requires reporting and notification but does not separately state a statewide parental-consent requirement.

Smith County Board of Education materials from June 2024 also reflect legislative guidance concerning Public Chapter 832 and recommend informing school employees of the new reporting obligation.

## What advocates and critics say

The Tennessee Equality Project describes Public Chapter 832 as an "outing" law and argues that mandatory disclosure can create privacy and safety risks for transgender and gender-diverse students.

In an October 2024 report, the organization said it collected de-identified responses from more than 50 people representing 24 Tennessee local education agencies. Respondents described a range of implementation practices, including email, written documentation, student-record or profile notes, and profile-alert practices.

Those survey results are advocacy-organization research, not official statewide data. Because the report intentionally de-identifies districts and respondents, the Tracker does not use it to attribute any specific electronic practice to Knox County or another named district.

## What is confirmed?

- Public Chapter 832 took effect May 1, 2024; targeted current-status searches did not identify a repeal or injunction.
- The statute requires a covered student accommodation request to move through a mandatory employee-to-administrator-to-parent reporting chain.
- The statute expressly connects some covered name requests to school registration forms and educational records.
- The law creates a private civil-enforcement mechanism for parents and separate enforcement authority for the Tennessee Attorney General.
- Knox County Schools documented a local implementation workflow involving staff reporting, parent contact, and a Title IX Gender Identity Accommodation Form.
- The Knox County memorandum does not require or reference an Aspen profile flag or automated SIS alert.
- Public Chapter 832 itself does not prescribe a statewide database, electronic form, portal, technical schema, or retention schedule.
- Targeted public searches through August 2026 did not identify a reported Tennessee court decision, injunction, public civil-enforcement action, or Attorney General enforcement action specifically under § 49-6-315. This is a negative search result, not proof that no such filing or action exists.

## What remains uncertain?

- Who can access a completed Knox County Title IX Gender Identity Accommodation Form in practice, beyond the general access rules that apply to education records.
- How long Knox County retains the form, whether it has a dedicated deletion or expungement rule, and whether the form is treated as part of a cumulative, behavioral, or other transferable student-record category.
- Whether and how Public Chapter 832 documentation is included when a Tennessee school transfers student records under Public Chapter 156 of 2025.
- Whether other Tennessee districts use email, paper forms, student-profile notes, SIS fields, parent portals, or other tools to implement the law.
- Whether a publicly accessible Tennessee School Boards Association model procedure or form specific to Public Chapter 832 exists.
- The exact wording of Wilson County's 2024-2025 handbook implementation language has not been independently preserved in the issue record.

## Who may be affected?

The law directly applies to Tennessee public-school employees, administrators, local education agencies, and public charter schools handling covered requests from students under 18.

Students who request covered gender-identity accommodations may have information about the request passed from the employee who receives it to school administration and then to a parent. Transgender and gender-diverse students are especially likely to encounter the law when asking school personnel to use a different name or pronouns or when requesting another accommodation covered by the statute.

Parents may receive notice under the statute and may have civil remedies if an LEA or public charter school does not comply. Teachers, counselors, principals, and other school personnel may also be affected by district procedures for documenting and escalating requests.

## Privacy and civil-liberties significance

Public Chapter 832 creates a government-required information flow involving a student's gender-identity-related request. The digital-rights significance is not that Tennessee created a statewide transgender-student database; the public record does not support that claim.

The significance is that implementation can create or rely on records, emails, forms, student profiles, or other school systems containing identity-related information, and that the statute requires some of that information to be disclosed to a parent.

If a Public Chapter 832 form, email, note, or other record is directly related to a student and maintained by a school, it may fall within FERPA's definition of an education record. FERPA generally gives parents, and later eligible students, rights to inspect education records and seek amendment of records they believe are inaccurate, misleading, or privacy-violating, subject to the regulation's requirements and exceptions.

Tennessee's 2025 student-record transfer law adds another unresolved question. If a district classifies Public Chapter 832 documentation as part of a transferable student-record category, the record may move to a receiving school. Current research has not established that classification for the Knox County Title IX form or for a comparable form in another named district.

## Lawful actions and resources

- Read Public Chapter 832 and the official Tennessee General Assembly bill history.
- Review your school district's student handbook, gender-accommodation guidance, student-record policy, and FERPA annual notice.
- Parents and eligible students can review the district's FERPA procedures for requesting access to or amendment of education records where those rights apply.
- Members of the public can request non-student-specific policies, forms, guidance, retention schedules, and administrative procedures under applicable public-records law. Student-specific education records are subject to privacy protections and should not be sought or republished unnecessarily.
- For advice about an individual student's rights or safety, consult a qualified attorney or an appropriate civil-rights or student-support organization.
