## Case scope

This Special Case asks a narrow question: **how are federal voter-data, citizenship-verification, and ballot-mail initiatives interacting with Tennessee's administration of the 2026 federal election?**

The case focuses on systems and data flows rather than campaign politics. Its core subjects are Tennessee's statewide voter-registration data, the federal Systematic Alien Verification for Entitlements program (SAVE), Tennessee's developing citizenship-verification portal, the Department of Justice's voter-roll collection effort, Executive Order 14399, and the Postal Service's 2026 ballot-mail rule.

The administration describes these initiatives as election-integrity measures intended to enforce existing federal law, prevent ineligible voting, improve voter-list accuracy, reduce mail-ballot fraud risk, and increase public confidence. Courts, state officials, voting-rights organizations, and privacy advocates have disputed parts of the federal government's legal authority, data practices, technical reliability, and timing.

This case does **not** attempt to establish that federal officials are planning to falsify election results, that Tennessee's election outcome is predetermined, or that every national election-policy dispute is connected to Tennessee. National developments are included only when they are necessary to understand a documented or conditional Tennessee data or election-system connection.

A 2024 USCIS record is included as a pre-scope baseline because it shows that Tennessee's use of SAVE for voter verification predates the 2025-2026 federal changes.

## Tennessee nexus

### Direct

Tennessee has several documented direct connections to the systems in this case:

- Tennessee election authorities were registered to use SAVE for voter registration and voter-list maintenance before the 2025 federal overhaul.
- Tennessee law authorizes the Coordinator of Elections to compare the statewide voter-registration database with SAVE and other government records for citizenship-verification purposes.
- Tennessee reported using the enhanced 2025 SAVE system to compare about four million voter records and referred 42 people identified as **potential** non-U.S. citizens who had voted to the FBI. The referrals were not final adjudications of citizenship or criminal liability.
- Tennessee provided its statewide voter-registration information to the U.S. Department of Justice. Secretary of State Tre Hargett and Elections Coordinator Mark Goins have directly confirmed the transfer.
- Tennessee declined DOJ's proposed voter-roll memorandum of understanding because, according to Goins, state officials were concerned the proposed terms could conflict with National Voter Registration Act removal rules and create false-positive removal risk.
- Tennessee enacted Public Chapter 473 in 2025 to create a county-facing citizenship-verification portal before January 1, 2028. Public Chapter 775 in 2026 authorizes that portal to incorporate SAVE through a secure federal web service if the service is made available.

A SAVE result does **not** itself automatically remove a Tennessee voter. Tennessee law controls the subsequent notice, proof, purge, and appeal process for a registered voter whose citizenship is questioned.

### Conditional

The following federal developments could materially affect Tennessee but are not presently established as operating against Tennessee voters:

- Executive Order 14399 directs DHS and SSA to take lawful and feasible steps toward compiling State Citizenship Lists and sending them to states before federal elections. The Supreme Court's August 24 opinion emphasized that the order does not require states to use those lists.
- USPS finalized new federal ballot-mail preparation and data-submission rules that could apply to Tennessee election offices if the current injunction against 2026 implementation is lifted.

### Context

A limited amount of national context is included to explain the federal architecture and litigation. Broader changes involving the Election Assistance Commission, CISA, federal election monitors, Fulton County's 2020 election materials, or other federal election controversies are **not treated as core evidence in this case unless a Tennessee operational effect is documented**.

## Current status

**As of August 24, 2026:**

Tennessee's direct voter-data and SAVE connections are established. Tennessee has long-standing statutory authority to use SAVE for voter citizenship verification, used the expanded federal system at large scale in 2025, and has a separate county-facing citizenship portal under development. The portal is not publicly established as production-live; an official fiscal note says it is being created and must be implemented before January 1, 2028.

The enhanced 2025 SAVE system that added bulk searching, Social Security Administration data, SSN-based searches, and records of natural-born citizens was vacated by a federal district court on June 22, 2026. The same court denied a stay on July 8. Tennessee is not among the four states that obtained separate restoration of the enhanced features in other litigation. An appeal remains pending. A same-day public-source recheck did not locate a later appellate order restoring those enhanced features generally for Tennessee.

Tennessee also transferred its statewide voter-registration information to DOJ. Public evidence strongly supports that the transferred data included names, dates of birth, addresses, and the last four digits of Social Security numbers; driver's-license or DMV identifiers are also strongly supported, although the exact Tennessee transmission manifest has not been located. No evidence reviewed establishes that full Social Security numbers were transmitted.

DOJ has described a general workflow in which collected state voter files can be processed through a DOJ-controlled SAVE account with DHS involvement. **No Tennessee-specific upload log, batch record, DHS request, or return-results file has been located showing that Tennessee's particular DOJ copy traveled through that federal SAVE pathway.** That connection remains unresolved.

Executive Order 14399 is in an unusual legal posture. On August 24, the Supreme Court stayed the injunction in `California v. Trump` while the appeal proceeds, largely on standing and ripeness grounds. The Court characterized the State Citizenship List directive as an internal federal instruction constrained by feasibility and applicable law, noted that states are not required by the order to use the lists, and expressly did not hold that later implementation measures will necessarily be lawful.

A **separate nationwide August 11 injunction in `League of Women Voters of Massachusetts v. Trump` remains the current practical barrier to USPS implementing Section 3 for the November 3, 2026 election or earlier federal elections.** USPS issued a final ballot-mail rule effective August 21, but the rule says USPS will not implement it for the 2026 election while the relevant injunctions remain. Plaintiffs have asked the district court to enforce that injunction after USPS finalized the rule; a late-August-24 public-source recheck did not locate a ruling resolving that enforcement motion.

The USPS final rule is narrower than a broad reading of the Executive Order. It does not direct USPS to determine a voter's citizenship or eligibility and does not establish a SAVE or State Citizenship List check by postal workers. Its new verification process applies to **outbound ballot mailings prepared by election officials before USPS accepts the batch**, not to a voter's completed return ballot.

No change to Tennessee's published absentee-return instructions from the federal USPS rule was located as of this review. Tennessee continues to instruct absentee voters to return completed ballots by mail, including USPS, FedEx, or UPS, in time for the county election commission to receive them by the close of polls on Election Day; hand delivery is not permitted.

## How the pieces connect

### Documented connections

**Tennessee voter data → DOJ.** Tennessee transferred statewide voter-registration information to DOJ after determining, based on state legal advice, that DOJ was entitled to the information. Tennessee did not sign the proposed DOJ MOU.

**Tennessee election administration → SAVE.** This connection predates the current administration. Tennessee law authorizes statewide voter-database comparisons with SAVE, and Tennessee used the enhanced system at large scale in 2025.

**Federal voter-roll collection → DOJ-described SAVE/DHS workflow.** DOJ's May 2026 OLC opinion and statements in federal litigation describe a general workflow in which collected voter data can be processed through a DOJ-controlled SAVE account with DHS involvement. The sources do not establish that every collected state file has been processed through that workflow.

**Tennessee county portal → possible future SAVE integration.** Public Chapter 775 expressly allows Tennessee's in-development citizenship portal to obtain SAVE information through a secure web service if USCIS makes it available.

**EO 14399 → USPS rulemaking.** The Executive Order directed USPS to pursue election-mail rulemaking; USPS subsequently finalized a ballot-mail rule.

### Supported relationships that require qualification

Tennessee's own SAVE use, DOJ's voter-roll collection effort, DOJ's described SAVE workflow, and EO 14399's State Citizenship List directive share a policy subject: using government records to assess citizenship or eligibility in election administration. The evidence does **not** establish that they are one unified technical system.

More specifically, DOJ has separately described a workflow for cross-checking collected voter information through SAVE, while EO 14399 separately directs DHS/SSA work on State Citizenship Lists, and Tennessee separately operates under its own state SAVE authority. The record does **not** establish that Tennessee's particular DOJ file was processed through DOJ's SAVE workflow, that it fed a State Citizenship List, or that Tennessee received match results from that copy.

### Possible relationships that are not established

No evidence reviewed establishes that EAC leadership changes, CISA staffing/program reductions, FBI actions involving 2020 Georgia election materials, federal election-monitor planning, or other national institutional developments directly changed Tennessee's 2026 voting systems or election cybersecurity. Those developments are therefore omitted from the core factual chain rather than used to make the chronology appear more connected than the evidence supports.

Public statements by political officials about mail voting, federal involvement in elections, or election fraud may provide political context. They do not by themselves prove agency causation, unlawful coordination, a plan to manipulate results, or the purpose of a specific Tennessee data transaction.

## What is confirmed?

- Tennessee election authorities were registered to use SAVE for voter verification before the 2025 federal overhaul.
- Tennessee law authorizes the Coordinator of Elections to compare statewide voter data with SAVE and establishes a notice/proof/appeal process when citizenship evidence concerning a registered voter is at issue.
- Tennessee used the enhanced 2025 SAVE system at statewide scale and referred 42 **potential** non-U.S.-citizen voters to the FBI; their final disposition is not established here.
- Tennessee is building a separate county-facing citizenship-verification portal, and 2026 law permits that portal to incorporate SAVE through a secure web service if available.
- The county-facing portal is not publicly established as production-live today.
- Tennessee transferred statewide voter-registration information to DOJ and declined DOJ's proposed MOU.
- DOJ has publicly described a general SAVE/DHS workflow for using collected state voter information.
- The enhanced 2025 SAVE bulk/SSA/SSN architecture was vacated in federal court; Tennessee is not part of the four-state restoration order identified in separate litigation.
- EO 14399 directs State Citizenship List work and USPS election-mail rulemaking.
- SCOTUS stayed one injunction against EO 14399 on August 24 but did not decide that all future implementation measures are lawful.
- A separate nationwide injunction still blocks USPS from implementing Section 3 for the November 2026 election at the review cutoff.
- USPS has finalized a rule, but the rule says it will not be implemented for the 2026 election while the relevant injunctions remain.
- The USPS final rule does not make USPS the decision-maker on voter citizenship or eligibility and does not apply its new acceptance-verification step to a voter's completed return ballot.

## What is not established?

The evidence reviewed does **not** establish that:

- federal officials have altered Tennessee vote totals or ballot tabulation;
- federal officials have a plan to fabricate, steal, cancel, or predetermine the 2026 election result;
- Tennessee's voter file sent to DOJ was actually uploaded into DOJ's SAVE workflow or directly transferred to HSI, USCIS, SSA, a contractor, or another federal entity;
- every one of Tennessee's 42 SAVE/FBI referrals was a noncitizen, was illegally registered, or committed voter fraud;
- SAVE itself automatically removes a Tennessee voter without a state notice and proof process;
- Tennessee's Public Chapter 473/775 county portal is currently live or that county election administrators currently hold direct SAVE credentials through it;
- Tennessee currently retains the enhanced bulk/SSA/SSN SAVE capabilities it used in 2025;
- Tennessee has received an EO 14399 State Citizenship List;
- USPS is presently refusing Tennessee absentee ballots under its new rule;
- USPS's final rule checks Tennessee voters against SAVE, a State Citizenship List, Social Security data, or a citizenship score;
- USPS would reject a voter's completed return ballot because the voter was absent from the Federal Ballot Mail Portal; or
- broader EAC, CISA, FBI, or federal-monitor developments have directly changed Tennessee's voting-machine operation or election-security services.

## What remains uncertain?

- What exact fields, file format, transmission method, retention terms, and Tennessee-specific privacy safeguards governed Tennessee's DOJ voter-data transfer?
- What does the missing Tennessee DOJ privacy/transmittal correspondence say?
- Was Tennessee's DOJ copy ever processed through the DOJ-controlled SAVE workflow, and did Tennessee receive any resulting matches?
- Which Tennessee personnel currently hold SAVE credentials and what roles, interfaces, logs, and permissions apply?
- What happened to the 42 people referred to the FBI after Tennessee's 2025 SAVE comparison?
- When will the county-facing citizenship portal become production-live, who is building or hosting it, and how will audit, outage, correction, and misuse controls work?
- What SAVE capabilities are available to Tennessee while the D.C. Circuit appeal proceeds?
- Has DHS created or transmitted a State Citizenship List for Tennessee under EO 14399?
- Will the separate USPS injunction remain in force through the period when Tennessee begins sending November absentee ballots?
- Will USPS, the First Circuit, the Supreme Court, or the district court materially change the final rule's legal status before the election?

## Digital-rights significance

This Special Case belongs in the Tennessee Digital Rights Tracker because its central questions concern **government data architecture and identity systems**.

Tennessee voter records containing sensitive identifying information have moved from a state election system into a federal department engaged in a national voter-roll collection effort. Tennessee also uses a federal citizenship-verification system whose capabilities expanded sharply in 2025 and were later limited by a federal privacy/statutory ruling. State law provides both existing statewide verification procedures and a new county-facing technical portal that may incorporate SAVE.

The digital-rights questions are therefore concrete: what information is transferred, what federal and state systems can query it, how matches are generated, what audit and access controls exist, how errors are corrected, when a database result can affect eligibility, and which institution is accountable at each step.

The ballot-mail component is also technical rather than merely rhetorical. If the current injunction were lifted, Tennessee election offices could face new outbound-envelope, barcode, and federal-portal requirements before USPS accepts federal ballot-mail batches. The final rule does **not** establish a federal citizenship-screening checkpoint for individual return ballots, but abrupt implementation could still create administrative dependencies for election offices close to an election.

- Tennessee announces production launch of the Public Chapter 473/775 portal;
- public records identify Tennessee SAVE users, system architecture, or audit/correction controls;
- records establish whether Tennessee's DOJ voter file was processed through SAVE/DHS;
- reliable records establish the disposition of the 42 Tennessee SAVE/FBI referrals;
- DOJ, DHS, EAC, CISA, or another federal body announces a Tennessee-specific election-system change that creates a direct technical or data connection.

## Lawful actions and resources

- **Check voter-registration status and election information through the Tennessee Secretary of State or the voter's county election commission.** Database questions and pending litigation do not change the value of verifying the state record directly.
- **For absentee voting, follow current Tennessee instructions and allow mailing time.** Tennessee requires the county election commission to receive a completed absentee ballot by the close of polls on Election Day.
- **Read the underlying primary documents.** The current legal status turns on specific court orders and the final USPS rule rather than social-media summaries of those documents.
- **Contact the Tennessee Division of Elections or a county election commission for implementation questions.** Public records requests can also be used to seek nonexempt policies, agreements, audit procedures, and system documentation.
- **For an individual eligibility or voting-rights dispute, consult a qualified attorney or nonpartisan voting-rights organization.** This Tracker documents systems and policy; it does not determine an individual's legal eligibility.
