# Tennessee Metric Media and 2026 Election Coverage

**Date:** 2026-08-19  
**Status:** Monitoring / working research memo  
**Publication status:** Not yet recommended as a public Tracker entry  
**Research question:** Are Tennessee-focused websites associated with Metric Media publishing or amplifying political communications in ways relevant to Tennessee's 2026 elections, and is there evidence that this activity constitutes a deliberate or coordinated election-influence operation?

## Current answer

**Election-related political-message amplification is confirmed. A coordinated Tennessee election-influence operation is not established.**

Independently reviewed evidence shows that Tennessee websites associated with the Metric Media ecosystem convert politicians' public communications, including social-media posts, into articles presented within geographically branded local-news websites. That activity continued during the 2025-2026 Tennessee election cycle.

The evidence reviewed so far does **not** establish that candidates, campaigns, PACs, or other political organizations paid for, commissioned, coordinated, or directed the identified coverage.

This memo is intentionally conservative. It distinguishes what was independently opened and verified from claims surfaced by AI-assisted discovery that still require direct verification.

---

## 1. Research provenance and evidence standard

This investigation used two independent AI-assisted discovery passes:

1. a ChatGPT research pass; and
2. a Perplexity Deep Research pass performed from a standalone prompt designed to search for both positive and negative evidence.

The Perplexity investigation's overall conclusion was **"Possibly — meaningful patterns but insufficient proof."** It identified Nashville Standard and Memphis Standard as the most politically relevant Tennessee sites and found election-salient content, but did not identify documentary evidence of a paid, coordinated, or formally organized Tennessee election-influence operation.

Under Tennessee Digital Rights Tracker methodology, AI-generated research is a **discovery source, not evidence by itself**. Accordingly, this memo uses the following categories:

- **Verified evidence:** the underlying webpage, dataset, official record, or source was opened and reviewed directly.
- **Research lead:** a potentially important claim or URL was surfaced by Perplexity or another discovery process but has not yet been independently verified to Tracker standards.
- **Unknown:** the available evidence does not currently answer the question.

No conclusion below should be stronger than the underlying evidence permits.

## 2. Network scope and terminology

### Current Tennessee roster

A Metric Media Tennessee directory reviewed during this investigation listed **20 Tennessee-branded publications**, including Clarksville Times, Hamilton Reporter, Johnson City Times, Kingsport Reporter, Knox Times, Rutherford Times, Shelby Reporter, Williamson News, and other regional titles.

Source: [Metric Media Tennessee directory](https://metric-media.pages.dev/tennessee/)

### Nashville Standard and Memphis Standard

Two of the most politically relevant sites identified during this investigation — **Nashville Standard** and **Memphis Standard** — were not part of the current 20-site Tennessee list reviewed above.

However, Columbia Journalism School's Tow Center dataset identifies both as part of the Metric Media Network.

Source: [Tow Center Metric Media domain dataset](https://github.com/TowCenter/partisan-local-news/blob/main/datasets/domains/metric_media/domains_metricmedia.csv)

An independently reviewed Nashville Standard article also displays a **"Metric Media Regional - Tennessee Network"** footer/network section, links to other Tennessee properties including Memphis Standard, and solicits support for the Metric Media Foundation.

Source: [Nashville Standard — Marsha Blackburn article](https://nashvillestandard.com/stories/674441743-sen-marsha-blackburn-addresses-crime-policy-supports-ice-and-praises-barrett-firearms-facility)

### Working terminology

To avoid overstating current ownership or operational status, this memo distinguishes:

- **Current officially listed Tennessee properties** — the 20 publications in the current Tennessee directory reviewed during this investigation.
- **Active Metric-associated or legacy Tennessee properties** — sites for which credible evidence establishes Metric Media network association but which do not appear in that current 20-site list.

For purposes of this memo, Nashville Standard and Memphis Standard are treated as **Metric-associated/legacy properties pending a dedicated current-ownership and network-status review**.

## 3. Verified finding: Nashville Standard converts political social-media messaging into local-news-style articles

An independently reviewed Nashville Standard article provides a direct example of the content-production mechanism.

### Marsha Blackburn example

The article **"Sen. Marsha Blackburn addresses crime policy, supports ICE, and praises Barrett Firearms facility"** is presented as a Nashville Standard local-government story. The article states that Blackburn posted a series of social-media messages and then reproduces or summarizes those positions concerning criminal justice, Tennessee manufacturing, immigration enforcement, and ICE.

Source: [Nashville Standard — Blackburn article](https://nashvillestandard.com/stories/674441743-sen-marsha-blackburn-addresses-crime-policy-supports-ice-and-praises-barrett-firearms-facility)

The important verified fact is not whether the article is favorable or unfavorable. It is that a geographically branded local-news site transformed a Tennessee political figure's own public communications into an article formatted as local political news.

### Assessment

**Verified:** Nashville Standard converts public social-media communications from Tennessee political figures into local-news-style articles.

**Not established:** whether the decision to publish any particular post was made to help or hurt a candidate, whether a campaign requested the article, or whether the article was paid for.

## 4. Verified finding: direct gubernatorial campaign messaging from Monty Fritts was republished as Nashville Standard news content

During the 2026 Republican gubernatorial primary, Nashville Standard published multiple articles built from candidate Monty Fritts' own social-media campaign messages.

### May 12, 2026

Nashville Standard published **"Monty Fritts outlines tax policy and social views in gubernatorial campaign tweets."** The article identified Fritts as a Tennessee gubernatorial candidate and reproduced campaign statements concerning taxes, law and order, religion, and related issues. One of the underlying posts contained a direct appeal to voters to vote for Fritts on August 6.

Source: [Nashville Standard — Fritts, May 12](https://nashvillestandard.com/monty-fritts-outlines-tax-policy-and-social-views-in-gubernatorial-campaign-tweets/)

### May 22, 2026

Nashville Standard published **"Monty Fritts outlines campaign priorities and criticizes rivals in gubernatorial race tweets."** The article reproduced campaign positions as well as criticism of Republican primary rivals Marsha Blackburn and John Rose.

Source: [Nashville Standard — Fritts, May 22](https://nashvillestandard.com/monty-fritts-outlines-campaign-priorities-and-criticizes-rivals-in-gubernatorial-race-tweets/)

### Assessment

**Verified:** Nashville Standard republished direct campaign persuasion and opponent criticism from a gubernatorial candidate during an active primary campaign.

This is stronger evidence of **electoral-message amplification** than generic issue coverage because the source material itself was explicitly campaign-oriented.

**Not established:** that Fritts, his campaign, an allied PAC, or another organization paid for, requested, coordinated, or knowingly participated in Nashville Standard's publication of the material.

## 5. Verified finding: TN-09 election messaging was republished by Nashville Standard

Nashville Standard also published election-specific congressional material.

The article **"Senator Brent Taylor voices support for Kevin Vaughan and criticizes Justin Pearson in TN-09 race"** reproduced public social-media statements involving:

- an endorsement of Kevin Vaughan;
- campaign positioning by Brent Taylor;
- criticism of Justin Pearson; and
- explicit discussion of the TN-09 congressional contest.

Source: [Nashville Standard — TN-09 article](https://nashvillestandard.com/senator-brent-taylor-voices-support-for-kevin-vaughan-and-criticizes-justin-pearson-in-tn-09-race/)

### Assessment

**Verified:** Nashville Standard transformed a public endorsement, candidate promotion, and opponent criticism tied to a named congressional race into a local-news-style article.

**Not established:** campaign coordination, payment, or candidate involvement in the site's publication process.

## 6. Verified finding: Blackburn generated election-salient source material during the 2026 campaign

Blackburn's official U.S. Senate website independently confirms that she was publicly promoting election-integrity and citizenship-verification policies during 2026.

On June 2, 2026, Blackburn's Senate office announced legislation intended to encourage states to submit voter-registration lists to the Department of Homeland Security's SAVE system. The official release emphasized citizenship requirements for voting, voter-roll maintenance, fraud prevention, and election integrity.

Source: [Office of Sen. Marsha Blackburn — Election Security Partnership Act](https://www.blackburn.senate.gov/2026/6/blackburn-graham-introduce-legislation-to-incentivize-every-state-to-submit-voter-rolls-to-dhs)

Perplexity surfaced several Nashville Standard URLs apparently built from Blackburn communications on these themes. Those exact pages should be recovered and archived before making a full article-level tone or asymmetry finding.

### Assessment

**Verified:** Blackburn was generating election-salient public communications of the type Nashville Standard demonstrably converts into news-style articles during the broader gubernatorial campaign period.

**Not yet verified:** the full text, framing, and relative prominence of every Blackburn article identified during AI-assisted discovery.

## 7. Verified finding: Memphis Standard also converts Democratic political communications into articles

An independent review of Memphis Standard found the same general production mechanism applied to a Democratic federal officeholder.

The Memphis Standard homepage carried an article titled **"Steve Cohen addresses immigration reporting directive and reflects on SCOTUS tradition."** The site's description stated that Rep. Steve Cohen used social-media posts dated June 18-19, 2026 to discuss an immigration-related Tennessee directive, Supreme Court traditions, and related political/legal matters.

Source: [Memphis Standard](https://memphisstandard.com/)

Cohen's official House communications independently document his public advocacy on Tennessee redistricting, voting representation, Summer EBT, and other issues that Perplexity identified as appearing in Memphis Standard coverage.

Representative official sources:

- [Rep. Steve Cohen — protest of Tennessee congressional redistricting](https://cohen.house.gov/media-center/press-releases/congressman-cohen-protests-reckless-plan-silence-memphis-black-vote)
- [Rep. Steve Cohen — Summer EBT statement](https://cohen.house.gov/media-center/press-releases/congressman-cohen-says-lee-plan-feed-children-inadequate)

Perplexity also identified the following Memphis Standard articles as research leads:

- [Steve Cohen criticizes Tennessee redistricting and defends Memphis representation](https://memphisstandard.com/steve-cohen-criticizes-tennessee-redistricting-and-defends-memphis-representation)
- [Steve Cohen criticizes Tennessee GOP redistricting efforts and Supreme Court leadership](https://memphisstandard.com/steve-cohen-criticizes-tennessee-gop-redistricting-efforts-and-supreme-court-leadership/)
- [Steve Cohen criticizes Supreme Court and state actions on Tennessee redistricting](https://memphisstandard.com/steve-cohen-criticizes-supreme-court-and-state-actions-on-tennessee-redistricting)

Several exact article pages were not fully retrievable during the independent verification pass, so their complete framing remains a research lead rather than a verified tone finding.

### Assessment

**Verified:** Memphis Standard uses public communications from a Democratic political figure as input for local-news-style articles.

This is an important constraint on interpretation. The mechanism is not demonstrated to operate only on behalf of Republican politicians.

## 8. Interpretive implication: the mechanism is broader than a simple one-party campaign operation

The independently verified examples involve:

- Republican political figures, including Marsha Blackburn and Monty Fritts; and
- a Democratic political figure, Steve Cohen.

That weakens any simple claim that every politically relevant Metric-associated article in Tennessee is necessarily part of a Republican candidate-support operation.

A plausible alternative explanation is that these sites operate a high-volume system that systematically or semi-systematically converts politicians' public communications into inexpensive locally branded content.

That explanation does **not** rule out partisan asymmetry, selective amplification, or political use by outside organizations. It means those propositions must be measured rather than assumed.

## 9. Local-race search: negative finding

One original concern was whether Tennessee Metric Media properties were already being used in low-information local elections such as sheriff, county mayor, school-board, judicial, or district-attorney races.

The Perplexity research pass specifically examined several local-race possibilities and did not identify a clearly documented cluster of candidate-specific Metric Media articles timed to manipulate a Tennessee local race in 2026.

For example, Clarksville Times coverage around a competitive Montgomery County sheriff election was largely focused on Sheriff's Office activity, crime, training, and community information rather than candidate persuasion.

### Assessment

**No meaningful evidence has yet been established of targeted Metric Media manipulation of a 2026 Tennessee sheriff, county mayor, school-board, district-attorney, or judicial race.**

This is a negative finding, not proof that no such activity exists.

**No payment or coordination evidence has been independently established during this research.**

That is different from asserting that no payment or coordination exists.

## 12. Evidence classification

### Confirmed

- A substantial Tennessee Metric Media network exists.
- A current Tennessee directory reviewed during this investigation listed 20 properties.
- Tow Center historical data identifies Nashville Standard and Memphis Standard as Metric Media Network properties.
- Nashville Standard publicly displays a Tennessee Metric Media network section and links to Memphis Standard and other Tennessee sites.
- Nashville Standard transforms politicians' public social-media posts into local-news-style articles.
- Nashville Standard republished direct 2026 gubernatorial campaign messaging from Monty Fritts.
- Nashville Standard republished election-specific TN-09 endorsement and opponent-criticism messaging.
- Memphis Standard also transforms political social-media communications into locally branded articles.
- Verified examples involve both Republican and Democratic political figures.
- This content-production mechanism operated during Tennessee's 2026 election cycle.

### Probable

The sites use a systematic or semi-systematic production process for transforming public political communications into inexpensive geographically branded content.

### Possible

- Candidate coverage may be asymmetric.
- Politically salient material may receive disproportionate amplification.
- Campaigns or outside political organizations could use these articles as secondary amplification assets.
- Some historically affiliated Tennessee sites may have changed ownership or network status while retaining similar production infrastructure.

These possibilities require direct testing.

### Unsupported at present

- Tennessee candidates paid Metric Media for the investigated coverage.
- Campaigns commissioned the identified articles.
- Metric Media coordinated publication with those campaigns.
- Nashville Standard deliberately suppressed Jerri Green or another candidate.
- Metric Media materially caused a Tennessee election result.
- The current 20-site Tennessee network was deployed as a coordinated 2026 election operation.
- Tennessee Metric Media activity is organizationally connected to white-nationalist groups.

## 14. Digital-rights significance

The strongest demonstrated concern at this stage is **information provenance and transparency**, not proven illegal election activity or covert campaign coordination.

A reader encountering a geographically branded publication such as Nashville Standard or Memphis Standard may reasonably interpret it as conventional independent local reporting.

The verified examples show that content within these sites can originate substantially from a politician's own social-media or official communications and then be reformatted as a news article.

That creates a concrete research question for civic information integrity:

> To what extent can candidate or officeholder messaging acquire the appearance of independent local reporting when converted into articles by a centralized or systematic digital publishing network?

That question becomes more significant in communities where traditional local reporting is limited and readers may have little information about how an article was produced.

Prior Tow Center research provides relevant national background on Metric Media's automation, political relationships, and local-news presentation model:

- [Tow Center — The Rise of Partisan Local Newsrooms](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center/the-rise-of-partisan-local-newsrooms.php)
- [Tow Center — Community Newsmaker / Metric Media](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center_reports/community-newsmaker-metric-media-local-news.php/)

The Tennessee evidence demonstrates that this infrastructure was actively reproducing election-related political messages in 2026.

What remains unknown is whether Tennessee political actors deliberately used the infrastructure as an electoral tool.

Build a complete dataset of candidate-related political coverage during the 2026 cycle and record:

- candidate;
- party;
- office/race;
- publication date;
- article title and URL;
- source type (tweet, press release, official statement, database, original reporting, etc.);

- headline framing;
- whether opponents are mentioned;

- Marsha Blackburn;

Determine whether candidates, campaigns, PACs, or political organizations:

- shared Metric-associated stories;
- linked to them;
- quoted them;
- promoted them through paid social advertising; or
- otherwise treated them as campaign assets.

### Priority 5 — Current ownership/network status

Determine the current legal and operational relationship between Metric Media and:

- Nashville Standard;
- Memphis Standard; and
- the currently listed Tennessee properties.

## 16. Publication recommendation

**Keep this memo in `research/` with status `Monitoring`. Do not publish it as a regular Tracker entry alleging an election-influence operation at this stage.**

The record is strong enough to preserve as a documented research lead and monitor through the November 2026 general election.

A public Tracker entry may become justified if later research establishes one or more of the following:

1. documented campaign or PAC payment;
2. documented campaign/publisher coordination;
3. systematic candidate asymmetry supported by quantitative analysis;
4. paid political amplification of Metric-produced articles;
5. evidence of misleading disclosure or provenance with a clear Tennessee civic/digital-rights consequence; or
6. another sufficiently strong state-level digital-rights nexus.

---

## Working conclusion

As of August 19, 2026, independently reviewed evidence establishes that **Tennessee websites associated with the Metric Media ecosystem convert political figures' public communications into articles presented through geographically branded local-news sites.**

Verified examples involve both Republican and Democratic political figures and include material directly relevant to Tennessee's 2026 election environment.

The evidence demonstrates **political-message amplification and an information-provenance concern**.

It does **not currently demonstrate a paid, coordinated, or Tennessee-specific election-influence operation**.

**Status: Monitoring.**
