# Research Note — EO 14399 and USPS legal-status matrix

This is an unpublished working evidence record supporting the possible Special Case **Federal Election Administration and Tennessee's 2026 Election**. It is not a public Tracker entry and is not publication-ready by itself.

This note addresses one narrow research pass: **what Executive Order 14399 directs, what USPS actually put in its final ballot-mail rule, what the courts have blocked or unblocked through August 24, 2026, and what—if anything—those measures are doing to Tennessee absentee voting right now.**

## Bottom line

As of late August 24, 2026:

1. The Supreme Court has **stayed the injunction in `California v. Trump`**, allowing the Executive Branch to continue trying to implement EO 14399 while the First Circuit appeal proceeds. The Supreme Court did **not** hold that downstream implementation measures are lawful and expressly said later measures may be challenged.
2. A **separate nationwide preliminary injunction entered August 11 in `League of Women Voters of Massachusetts v. Trump` remains in force** and blocks USPS from implementing Section 3 of EO 14399 for the November 3, 2026 election or any earlier federal election.
3. USPS nevertheless issued a final rule effective August 21. The rule itself acknowledges the injunctions and says USPS will **not implement it for the 2026 election unless and until the government obtains relief**. After the Supreme Court's August 24 action, the separate League injunction still prevents 2026 implementation.
4. The final USPS rule is materially narrower and different from the strongest reading of the Executive Order. It does **not** have USPS determine citizenship or voter eligibility, does **not** cross-check voters against a federal State Citizenship List, and does **not** apply its acceptance-verification process to completed return ballots mailed by voters.
5. If eventually allowed to operate for 2026, the final rule would regulate **outbound ballot mailings prepared by election officials**: envelope standards, unique Intelligent Mail barcodes, a Federal Ballot Mail Portal, and state-supplied name/address/barcode information. A noncompliant outbound mailing could be rejected before USPS accepts it, with election officials responsible for curing the problem and resubmitting.
6. Tennessee absentee voters are therefore **not currently subject to the new USPS rule**. Tennessee's existing rule remains that a voter must return an absentee ballot by mail/carrier in time for the county election commission to receive it by close of polls on Election Day.
7. EO 14399's State Citizenship List directive is no longer blocked by the `California` injunction, but its actual implementation remains uncertain and legally constrained. A separate D.D.C. judgment vacated the 2025 modified SAVE bulk/SSA/SSN architecture, and no public record located in this pass establishes that Tennessee has received a State Citizenship List.

## Finding 1 — SCOTUS unblocked one injunction, not the whole election order

On August 24, 2026, the Supreme Court granted the federal government's stay application in `Trump v. California`, No. 26A124. The majority concluded the state plaintiffs were unlikely to have standing/ripeness to challenge the Executive Order itself at that stage.

The majority characterized § 2(a) as an internal directive: the states are not required to use a State Citizenship List, and future implementation is conditioned on feasibility and applicable law. It similarly treated § 2(b) as an instruction to prioritize enforcement of existing federal laws and § 3 as an internal rulemaking directive for purposes of standing measured when the complaint was filed.

The Court explicitly preserved later legal challenges. It stated that its stay does **not** mean an implementation measure will necessarily be lawful and said that if a USPS final rule harms states, the rule can be challenged.

### Correct Tracker phrasing

Use:

> The Supreme Court stayed one injunction on justiciability grounds, allowing the Executive Branch to continue trying to implement EO 14399 while the appeal proceeds. It did not decide that every implementation measure is lawful.

Do **not** use:

> The Supreme Court upheld Trump's mail-voting restrictions.

That overstates the holding.

## Finding 2 — the August 11 USPS injunction is the key current guardrail

In the separate `League of Women Voters of Massachusetts v. Trump` case, Judge Indira Talwani on August 11 preliminarily enjoined USPS defendants from implementing, giving effect to, or enforcing Section 3 of EO 14399 with respect to the November 3 election or any earlier federal election.

The order specifically includes:

- refusing to transmit mail-in or absentee ballots; and
- initiating or completing rulemaking to promulgate the specified § 3(b)(i)-(v) or § 3(d) regulations for the 2026 midterm/elections before it.

It allows nonbinding USPS ballot-envelope guidance.

The August 24 Supreme Court order addressed the **different state case** and stayed that state-case injunction. It did not vacate the League preliminary injunction. Plaintiffs/counsel stated August 24 that no party had sought a stay of the League order.

### Correction to an earlier working assumption

Earlier discussion treated the government's August 12 Supreme Court filing as if it were a separate stay request directed at the League injunction. On closer review, the current evidence does **not** support that characterization. The League plaintiffs state no party has sought a stay of their August 11 order. The dossier/public case should use the corrected procedural posture.

## Finding 3 — USPS finalized the rule while the League injunction remained in force

USPS's final rule is Federal Register document **2026-17238**, titled `Ballot Mail for Federal Elections`.

The public-inspection version says:

- scheduled Federal Register publication: August 26, 2026;
- effective date: August 21, 2026;
- an immediate effective date was selected in order to preserve the possibility of 2026 implementation; and
- USPS will **not take actions to implement the rule specifically for the 2026 election unless and until the government obtains relief from the relevant injunctions**.

When that text was drafted, both the `California` and `League` injunctions were in place. SCOTUS has now stayed the former. The latter remains.

Therefore the most defensible current status is:

> **Finalized/effective on paper, but enjoined and non-operational for the 2026 election.**

Plaintiffs filed an emergency motion August 22 asking Judge Talwani to enforce the injunction, arguing that merely issuing/finalizing the rule violated the prohibition on completing 2026 rulemaking. At this research cutoff, that motion has not been resolved on the merits.

## Finding 4 — the final USPS rule is materially narrower than the Executive Order's strongest wording

The original EO said the proposed rule should specify that USPS **“shall not transmit”** mail ballots from any individual unless the person was enrolled on a state-specific participation list.

That sentence understandably suggested a system in which USPS might inspect individual voter eligibility or refuse an individual voter's completed ballot.

The **final rule does something importantly different**.

### What states/election officials would upload

For each person receiving a federal mail/absentee ballot, an authorized state/local portal user would submit:

- name;
- mailing address;
- unique Intelligent Mail barcode on the outbound envelope;
- unique Intelligent Mail barcode on the return envelope; and
- originating election-office state.

The final rule does **not** list SSN, date of birth, citizenship data, SAVE results, or State Citizenship List status as Portal fields.

### What USPS says it will check

Before accepting an **outbound ballot mailing from an election official**, USPS would check that the required portal information exists and that the mailing meets envelope/barcode standards.

It expressly says USPS will not:

- determine whether the name is substantively correct;
- assess address validity for this verification;
- assess voter eligibility; or
- assess citizenship.

States remain responsible for deciding who is eligible and for the contents of the participant list.

### What happens when an outbound batch is noncompliant

A mailing failing the pre-acceptance verification would not be accepted. USPS would return it to the **authorized ballot mailer**—the election official/mailer—for correction and resubmission.

This creates a potentially important operational dependency if the rule is ever activated shortly before an election, but it is different from USPS independently disqualifying voters.

## Finding 5 — completed return ballots are not screened under the final rule

This is the largest factual correction from our earlier working description.

The final DMM text says the outbound verification rule **does not apply to Return Federal Ballot Mail**.

USPS further explains that a voter may continue to return a completed ballot through a residential mailbox, blue collection box, or retail counter. Once return ballot mail enters the mailstream, it is processed/delivered under current operational practices.

The rule also states that after an **outbound** ballot mailing has been accepted, it will proceed through the mailstream and will not be intercepted, returned, or delayed because of the rule.

### Correct Tracker implication

Do **not** say:

> USPS's final rule will reject a Tennessee voter's completed ballot if that voter is not on a federal eligibility list.

The final rule does not establish that mechanism.

A more accurate concern is:

> If the injunction were lifted and the final rule activated, Tennessee election officials using USPS for outbound absentee ballots would have to comply with new federal envelope, barcode, and Portal-submission requirements before USPS accepted those outbound ballot batches. Late or erroneous implementation could therefore affect how quickly ballots are sent to voters, even though USPS would not adjudicate those voters' eligibility or newly screen their completed return ballots.

## Finding 6 — no direct final-rule connection to State Citizenship Lists or SAVE was located

EO 14399 places State Citizenship Lists and mail-ballot rulemaking in the same broader executive-order architecture. The President's stated rationale also linked citizenship verification and ballot-mail controls.

But the **final USPS rule itself** does not make the Postal Service's participation list a copy of the State Citizenship List and does not direct USPS to run Portal names through SAVE or SSA.

The final rule instead relies on states/election officials to decide who receives a ballot and to upload the mailpiece/recipient information.

Therefore the present connection should be classified:

| Proposed relationship | Classification |
|---|---|
| EO 14399 contains both federal citizenship-list and USPS initiatives | **Documented connection — same order / coordinated policy architecture** |
| Final USPS rule uses a State Citizenship List to approve Tennessee absentee voters | **Not established / contradicted by final-rule design** |
| Final USPS Portal queries SAVE/SSA to decide voter citizenship | **Not established** |
| State election officials determine who receives a ballot and provide Portal data | **Documented final-rule design** |

This narrowing makes the public Special Case stronger: it prevents us from turning two adjacent federal systems into a data flow that the operative rule does not establish.

## Finding 7 — State Citizenship Lists are less legally blocked after SCOTUS, but not proven operational in Tennessee

EO § 2(a) directs DHS/USCIS and SSA to compile State Citizenship Lists from federal citizenship/naturalization records, SSA data, SAVE data, and other federal databases and to transmit them at least 60 days before a regularly scheduled federal election. For the November 3 election, that points to approximately **September 4, 2026**.

The Supreme Court's August 24 stay removes the `California` injunction that had blocked implementation as to the plaintiff states and required reversal of implementation steps.

But several important limits remain:

- the EO itself says implementation must be feasible and consistent with applicable law;
- the Supreme Court did not decide the legality of a future list or implementation measure;
- the states are not required by the EO/majority's reading to use the lists;
- the separate D.D.C. judgment vacating the 2025 modified SAVE system remains a constraint on the enhanced bulk/SSA/SSN system Tennessee used in 2025; and
- this pass found no public Tennessee-specific record establishing receipt of an EO 14399 State Citizenship List.

For the Tracker, the correct current label is therefore:

> **EO-level implementation unblocked; actual list creation/transmission and Tennessee receipt remain uncertain.**

## Finding 8 — Tennessee's absentee process makes USPS operationally important, but nothing changed tonight

Tennessee Secretary of State guidance says:

- absentee-by-mail requests generally may be made no earlier than 90 days and no later than 10 days before an election;
- completed absentee ballots must be returned by mail/carrier rather than hand-delivered; and
- the county election commission must receive the ballot by the close of polls on Election Day.

That means postal reliability and outbound timing matter materially to Tennessee absentee voters.

However, because the August 11 League injunction remains in force, **the new federal USPS rule is not an operative Tennessee requirement for the November 2026 election as of this research cutoff**.

If the injunction is later stayed/lifted, the risk would primarily be a last-minute change imposed on Tennessee **election officials' outbound ballot-mail preparation and Portal workflow**, not a new USPS citizenship adjudication of individual Tennessee voters' completed return ballots.

## Corrections to earlier working language

The following earlier shorthand should **not** survive into public publication:

1. **“The Supreme Court upheld Trump's mail-ballot restrictions.”**  
   Correction: SCOTUS stayed one injunction based on standing/ripeness and expressly left legality of implementation measures open.

2. **“The Supreme Court lifted the USPS injunction.”**  
   Correction: it stayed the `California` injunction; the separate nationwide August 11 `League` USPS injunction remains.

3. **“The administration separately asked SCOTUS to stay the August 11 League injunction.”**  
   Correction: current evidence does not support that statement; League plaintiffs state no party has sought a stay of their injunction as of Aug. 24.

4. **“USPS's final rule checks citizens against federal citizenship lists.”**  
   Correction: final rule expressly says USPS does not assess citizenship or voter eligibility and lists no SAVE/SSA/citizenship field in the Portal submission.

5. **“USPS would refuse to deliver completed return ballots from voters who aren't on its list.”**  
   Correction: final rule's acceptance verification applies only to outbound election-official mailings; completed return ballots continue through ordinary USPS channels.

6. **“USPS would refuse all ballots from a noncompliant state.”**  
   Correction: the rule's immediate enforcement mechanism is rejection of a **noncompliant outbound ballot mailing before acceptance**, with the authorized ballot mailer able to correct and resubmit it.
