# Adversarial review — Federal Election Administration and Tennessee's 2026 Election

This is an unpublished review of `federal-election-administration-2026-public-draft.md`. Its purpose is to challenge the draft from skeptical, legal, technical, and editorial perspectives before any content is moved into `_special_cases/`.

Review cutoff: late August 24, 2026 Central Time.

## Review verdict

**Verdict: suitable for revision toward publication. No fatal evidentiary defect found.**

The draft has a strong Tennessee nexus and is substantially better supported than the original broad hypothesis. The central factual chain is reproducible:

1. Tennessee's election use of SAVE predates 2025.
2. Tennessee used the enhanced 2025 SAVE system at statewide scale.
3. Tennessee separately transferred statewide voter information to DOJ.
4. DOJ has described a general voter-file-to-SAVE/DHS cross-checking workflow.
5. Tennessee's specific DOJ file has **not** been proven to have traveled through that workflow.
6. Tennessee is building a separate county citizenship portal that may later integrate SAVE; the portal is not established as live.
7. EO 14399 created separate federal State Citizenship List and USPS rulemaking tracks.
8. SCOTUS stayed one injunction on Aug. 24, but a separate Aug. 11 USPS injunction remains the practical 2026 blocker.
9. USPS's final rule regulates outbound election-office mail preparation and does not establish citizenship screening of individual return ballots.

The strongest editorial feature is the draft's explicit separation of documented connections from unresolved ones. The greatest remaining risk is not lack of evidence but **accidentally implying one unified federal/Tennessee technical pipeline where the sources establish several related but partly separate systems.**

## Challenge 1 — Are we implying that Trump or EO 14248 created Tennessee's SAVE relationship?

**Finding:** The draft correctly says no. USCIS documentation identifies Tennessee as a voter-verification SAVE user before the 2025 overhaul.

**Revision needed:** The timeline row for March 25, 2025 currently places EO 14248 and the later SAVE expansion in one sentence. That could imply the order itself directly created every later SAVE modification. Separate or narrow that wording. The D.D.C. record establishes the modified SAVE architecture; it should not be used to infer causation beyond the evidence.

## Challenge 2 — Are we turning DOJ's general SAVE plan into proof Tennessee's file was processed?

**Finding:** The draft repeatedly says the Tennessee-specific path is unresolved. Good.

**Revision needed:** Phrases such as "DOJ has documented a general federal architecture for processing collected state voter files" are defensible but could sound like completed processing of all collected files. Prefer "DOJ has described a general workflow for processing collected state voter files" or "DOJ has documented its intended/claimed workflow."

Likewise, replace broad synthesis such as "federal policy architecture aimed at combining voter-roll information with federal citizenship-verification capabilities" with a more precise statement: DOJ has described a voter-file/SAVE workflow; EO 14399 separately directs State Citizenship Lists; Tennessee separately has its own SAVE authorities. A unified end-to-end technical system is not established.

## Challenge 3 — Are we overstating Tennessee's transferred fields?

**Finding:** Completed transfer itself is directly confirmed by Hargett and Goins. Names, DOB, addresses, and last-four SSN are consistently reported. Driver/DMV identifiers are strongly supported but the actual Tennessee field manifest remains missing.

**Revision needed:** Keep the exact-field qualification prominent. Do not shorten "last four digits of Social Security numbers" to "SSNs." Do not state full SSNs were transferred. Driver/DMV identifiers should remain qualified until the manifest/transmittal is located.

## Challenge 4 — Are we treating the 42 Tennessee SAVE referrals as proven illegal voters?

**Finding:** No. The draft repeatedly uses "potential" and says final disposition is unresolved.

**Revision needed:** None substantive. Preserve this wording. A future disposition search could improve the case but is not a publication prerequisite.

## Challenge 5 — Are we treating SAVE as an automatic purge system?

**Finding:** No. The draft identifies Tennessee's notice/proof/appeal process.

**Revision needed:** Consider adding one explicit sentence in the public `Current status` or `Tennessee nexus` section: a SAVE result does not by itself remove a Tennessee voter; state law controls the subsequent notice and eligibility process. This is a material guardrail and should not be buried.

## Challenge 6 — Are we treating the county portal as operational?

**Finding:** No. The draft accurately distinguishes existing statewide SAVE use from the Public Chapter 473/775 county portal and cites the fiscal note saying the portal is being created.

**Revision needed:** None substantive. Keep "not publicly established as production-live" rather than categorical claims about internal development state beyond the fiscal note.

## Challenge 7 — Are we overstating what SCOTUS decided on Aug. 24?

**Finding:** No. The draft accurately characterizes the stay as focused on standing/ripeness and preserves the Court's warning that future measures may still be unlawful/challengeable.

**Revision needed:** Add that the Court characterized State Citizenship Lists as not mandatory for states to use and conditioned federal creation/transmission on feasibility and applicable law. This is important narrowing context.

## Challenge 8 — Does the Aug. 24 Supreme Court order eliminate the separate USPS injunction?

**Finding:** No. The draft correctly says the Aug. 11 `League` injunction remains the practical blocker. A late-Aug. 24 recheck found no later ruling on the plaintiffs' motion to enforce and the League's public statement continues to say its injunction remains in place.

**Revision needed:** Preserve a hard publication requirement to recheck the docket immediately before merge. This legal status is unusually volatile.

## Challenge 9 — Are we inaccurately describing the USPS final rule as a citizenship-screening system?

**Finding:** No. The draft correctly reflects the final rule: USPS does not decide voter eligibility/citizenship; the portal fields are mailing/recipient/barcode data; the new acceptance-verification step applies to outbound election-official mailings rather than completed return ballots.

**Revision needed:** Keep this correction prominent because it materially narrows earlier concerns. Avoid language suggesting postal workers compare individuals against SAVE or State Citizenship Lists unless a later rule/system record changes the architecture.

## Challenge 10 — Are we overstating Tennessee's present absentee-voting change?

**Finding:** The final USPS rule is not currently operating for 2026 under the separate injunction. Tennessee SOS continues to instruct voters to return absentee ballots by mail (including USPS/FedEx/UPS) in time for receipt by the county election commission by close of polls.

**Revision needed:** Replace "Tennessee's existing absentee-voting rule therefore remains unchanged today" with the more evidence-bounded statement: "No change to Tennessee's published absentee-return instructions from the federal USPS rule was located as of the research cutoff." This avoids claiming exhaustive knowledge of every internal election-office procedure.

## Challenge 11 — Do EAC, CISA, FBI/Fulton County, monitors, grant conditions, or political rhetoric belong in the core chain?

**Finding:** Not on the current evidence. They may be relevant national context, but no sufficiently direct Tennessee operational effect has been established for the public case.

**Decision:** **Omit from the core timeline and core findings.** It is appropriate to mention in `What is not established?` that the research did not prove a Tennessee operational connection. This reduces narrative stacking and protects the case from a "guilt by chronology" criticism.

CISA can remain an update trigger because Hargett has acknowledged past Tennessee interaction with CISA, but the current effect of federal reductions is unresolved.

## Challenge 12 — Are we fairly representing the government's stated rationale?

**Finding:** Mostly yes. The draft says the administration describes the measures as enforcing federal law, preventing ineligible voting, improving list accuracy, reducing mail-ballot fraud risk, and increasing confidence. It also separately records legal/privacy/technical objections.

**Revision needed:** No major change. Avoid attributing critics' motive claims as facts. Likewise, do not present the administration's fraud rationale as proof that fraud is common.

## Challenge 13 — Could this be criticized as a general anti-Trump politics page rather than a Tennessee digital-rights case?

**Finding:** The draft is now sufficiently technical and Tennessee-specific. Its strongest material concerns data transfer, SAVE architecture, state portal design, correction procedures, and postal mail-processing rules. It intentionally excludes many politically salient national events that lack a Tennessee system connection.

**Decision:** Scope is justified under `election-systems-data`.

## Challenge 14 — Is the confidence level too high or too low?

**Finding:** `Medium` is appropriate. Many individual components are high-confidence, but the case's central cross-system relationship still contains meaningful unknowns: exact DOJ file manifest, Tennessee-specific DOJ→SAVE processing, account/access controls, State Citizenship List receipt, and rapidly changing litigation.

## Challenge 15 — What would falsify or materially weaken the case's broader synthesis?

The case should be revised if evidence establishes any of the following:

- Tennessee's DOJ transfer contained only ordinary public voter-file fields and not the sensitive identifiers currently reported;
- Tennessee's DOJ copy was contractually firewalled from SAVE/DHS use under terms not yet located;
- DOJ abandoned or was legally barred from applying the voter-file/SAVE workflow to Tennessee;
- Tennessee did not use enhanced SAVE in the manner described by state reporting/official correspondence;
- the county portal is cancelled or materially redesigned so SAVE cannot be integrated;
- the USPS final rule is withdrawn, superseded, permanently enjoined, or materially rewritten; or
- a later court order changes the operative status described here.

None of those possibilities undermines the already-established fact that Tennessee voter data moved to DOJ or that Tennessee has used SAVE for voter verification; they would change the significance and connections among the components.

## Publication blockers after revision

The broad research does **not** need another open-ended national sweep before drafting. The remaining blockers are procedural and current-status focused:

1. Revise the public-format draft using the points above.
2. Draft at least one linked ordinary Tennessee Tracker entry; the Tennessee→DOJ voter-roll transfer is the strongest candidate.
3. Immediately before publication, recheck:
   - the Aug. 11 `League` injunction and motion to enforce;
   - any First Circuit or Supreme Court action affecting USPS Section 3;
   - the D.C. Circuit modified-SAVE appeal;
   - Tennessee SOS implementation/absentee guidance;
   - any confirmed State Citizenship List transmission to Tennessee.
4. Move the revised public-format draft to `_special_cases/` only after the linked entry exists.
5. Run repository validation and final source-link review.

## Final adversarial assessment

The case survives adversarial review **if it is published as an evidence map with visible uncertainty, not as proof of an election-rigging conspiracy**.

The most defensible public conclusion is narrower:

> Tennessee has direct, documented connections to a broader federal election-data and citizenship-verification policy environment through its own SAVE use and its transfer of statewide voter information to DOJ. DOJ has separately described a federal voter-file/SAVE workflow, and EO 14399 separately directs State Citizenship Lists and election-mail rulemaking. Some of these systems share data-governance and eligibility-verification purposes, but the evidence does not establish one unified Tennessee-to-federal pipeline or a plan to manipulate election results.

That conclusion is supported by the evidence currently in the dossier and remains appropriately falsifiable as new records emerge.
